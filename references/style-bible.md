# Style Bible

## Palette

- Sky/atmosphere: muted cyan, pale cream, deep blue.
- Roof/wood: terracotta, umber, warm ochre.
- Wing: cream canvas, walnut ribs, muted red binding.
- Wind cue: saturated red-orange ribbon.
- Character: warm pale face, teal vest, cream shirt, dark trousers, brown leather.
- Climax: golden sunset edge light over cool blue water.

## Set construction

Use 3–5 depth layers: foreground roof tile/parapet, action plane, secondary rooftops and tools, horizon/water, and atmosphere. Keep the character readable against the set. Use large foreground materials to make the camera feel inside a designed world rather than in front of a flat background.

## Camera grammar

Alternate centered medium explanation, close prop proof, reaction, technical top-down or POV, and wide geographic consequence. Use clean digital pushes, motivated pans, internal prop fly-throughs, and hard cuts on action. Keep ordinary exposition stable; reserve shake for a failure.

## Character and prop grammar

Use a limited puppet with pivoted shoulders, elbows, hips, and knees. Favor clear silhouette and readable holds. The wing is the hero continuity object and must appear inert, assembled, tested, flexing, and consequential. The red ribbon and brass gauge explain invisible wind and angle changes.

## Caption grammar

When captions are requested, use short white sans-serif phrases with dark outline/shadow, centered low but above the safe margin. Prefer 1–3 words per beat. Keep the long narration as a separate SRT; do not force full sentences onto the picture.
